# Calendar booking automations, 27 October 2026

## Decision and scope

The owner rejected working-hour blocking as a major sales scene, then asked what could earn the removed space. A standalone intake-form replacement was challenged because customer context needs a clear source. The approved direction is a compact configured booking-to-message sequence, with form answers and existing CRM history as supporting context.

Keep the existing Calendar, five formats, Meeting Rooms, saved recording/transcript, Guided Launch/FAQ and final CTA structure. The new scene sits inside the existing business chapter before the video meeting. It replaces the opening's duplicate reminder with a clear booking-form question/answer and retains its on-screen confirmation. Native calendar marks stay below booking, and basic availability stays in the FAQ. No shared shell, Platform architecture, route/indexing, `/booking`, dependency or unrelated page change.

- REJECT another isolated settings/form sales panel, a repeated reminder, a third broad green surface, pastel tiles, a feature catalogue, fake email addresses, delivery ticks, decorative controls or an unsupported Reschedule button.

Buyer question: what does Zapla do around a confirmed appointment? Starting fact: Emma has supplied her name and a specific kitchen/benchtop answer through the visible booking form. The business configures the booking event, email, offset and SMS. The customer receives appointment details then a reminder. The website explains this configuration; it does not activate it, generate a new summary, infer the customer's intent or send a message. The sceptical misread that every booking defaults to these exact messages is addressed by the adjacent configuration copy and FAQ. These boundaries are supported separately in the Calendar/Automation evidence files.

One short desktop reveal advances booking, email and reminder, then holds. It starts only when the scene is mostly visible, excludes offscreen/hidden-tab time, has no looping reset or playback toolbar and takes 4.2 seconds. The complete content is the server-rendered default and remains normally readable on small/short/reduced-motion layouts. Only opacity and a small body translation change; source, headings, dimensions and reading order remain stable. There is no scroll capture or automatic navigation.
