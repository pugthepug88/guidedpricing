# Mortgage brokers page review

Route: `/industries/mortgage-brokers`

## Direction

The page owns the long client relationship: an agreed check-in before readiness, relevant reconnection with an older enquiry, and broker approved review invitations after settlement. It extends the incumbent Zapla visual language without replacing the shared navigation or footer.

USE: actual Zapla identity, existing local fonts, portrait system and root navigation/footer.
ADAPT: the incumbent industry's restrained human context and short causal scenes for a relationship that unfolds over months rather than a workshop booking.
REJECT: replacing specialist mortgage software, generic AI advice, fabricated returns or testimonials, native integration promises, decorative dashboard chrome and looping customer state.

## October 12 holistic review: current ratings

This section supersedes the earlier blanket 9/10 table. Reviewed source commit 6b5d7d771b225255980ec66f078e3744fd0065a8, also returned as Lovable's current commit. These are one author's critical assessments, not separate experts or independent certifications. Overall judgment: 7.5/10 as a strategic industry sales page. Strong visual execution is not proof of a persuasive paid offer.

### Inspection

Read the current implementation and inspected rendered whole pages and zoomed-out layouts for Mortgage Brokers, homepage, Dental, Mechanics, Follow-Up and Customer Marketing. Current mortgage render at 1440 CSS pixels: 7167px total including footer, 6226px main page. Hero/recognition/prospect occupy roughly the first third of the main page; mortgage relationship demonstrations end around 3030px. The following fit, implementation, pricing, FAQ and final call consume the remainder. Mobile at 390px is 9863px including footer. The point is emphasis and content value, not a universal page-length limit.

320, 390 and 768 widths had no horizontal overflow. The fixed-rate toggle and keyboard FAQ worked. Reduced-motion mortgage run had no page/hydration errors. Normal prospect reached state 2 after 1.8s. Full-page homepage captures contain scroll-pinned runway; this was not misclassified as an ordinary blank content section.

### Strategic role within the website

Homepage owns the broad promise and platform. Follow-Up owns active-opportunity execution. Reactivation owns reopening dormant opportunities. Customer Marketing owns relevant audience, timing and campaign activity. The mortgage page should translate those capabilities into a brokerage's client lifecycle and qualify a real paid fit beside specialist lending software. It should not present itself as a full replacement mortgage CRM.

Keep the current hero, editorial imagery and post-settlement focus. Three commercially distinct stories should carry the page: active prospects not ready yet, eligible enquiries already dormant, and settled clients due for a review invitation. The current page fully demonstrates the first and third, but treats the second as a supporting link.

### Material findings and priority

1. Differentiation is the main weakness. BrokerEngine's primary documentation already describes scheduled reviews, fixed rate expiry triggers and workflow automation. The defensible hypothesis is configured execution of gaps in a particular brokerage, not unique reminder functionality. Explain what Zapla gets working, how replies are handled and what staff still maintain.
2. Hero, recognition and prospect repeat the not-ready story. Shorten recognition, then use the space for a distinct eligible older-enquiry reactivation demonstration. Do not add extra decorative photos merely to increase section count.
3. Show the team's next action. Illustrative client messages explain outreach but not the broker's working view: who needs contact, why now, who owns the response and how the prior history helps. Any new demo must be labelled illustrative and respect actual capability verification.
4. The homepage's Richard K broker story states 4 deals signed in 17 days from an older contact database. That use case is underrepresented on this page. The presence of the claim in source is not verification. Confirm its evidence and approval before using it here; otherwise use honest mechanism proof.
5. At current quoted starting prices, first-year platform plus Guided Launch starts at A$6,785 for Follow-Through and A$11,385 for Growth, excluding GST and usage. The page must earn that cost by showing incremental useful conversations and staff responsibilities. Do not invent a per-settlement commission or projected return.
6. Mortgage Brokers is absent from both desktop and mobile industry links in SiteNav, although other industries are listed. Restore its discovery path as part of website integration. Intentional noindex is correct for a draft; change only as part of production launch readiness.
7. The plan descriptions are generic despite the industry context. Map the initial enquiry/check-in flows to Follow-Through and ongoing database reactivation/review invitations to the agreed Growth scope without silently inventing inclusions.
8. Retain the existing visual direction. A full lending-software pitch was considered and rejected: it would broaden unsupported claims, increase perceived migration cost and weaken the honest role beside incumbent systems. A narrower, commercially complete relationship page is the recommended alternative.
